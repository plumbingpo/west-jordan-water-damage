Wet Carpet Drying in West Jordan

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. For West Jordan homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

What It Looks Like

  • The room smells sour and stale, especially after it has been closed for a while
  • A wet spot that keeps growing even though no new water has been added
  • Backing that separates from the carpet when you lift a corner to look
  • Socks that come away damp, or a squishing sound, when you cross the carpet
  • Carpet that still feels cold and clammy to bare feet days after the spill
  • Under the wet section, the floor gives a little or squeaks when you step on it
How We Fix It

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Find the Edges

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Pull Out the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry Every Layer

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Soaked Pad Underneath

A carpet pad absorbs many times what it weighs in water. The carpet over it and the floor under it keep getting damp from the pad, even once the surface seems dry.

Subfloor Damage

Left wet, the OSB or particleboard beneath a carpet swells and then crumbles. The soft spots, squeaking and lifted seams appear weeks afterward, when nobody remembers the spill.

Clean Water Turns Dirty

Even clean water goes bad the longer it sits. It collects bacteria and dirt from the pad and fibers, which can turn a simple drying job into a removal.

Mold on the Backing

Growth usually starts on the underside of the carpet, where it is dark and damp. The odor arrives a long time ahead of any visible sign on top.

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Moves Outward

Under the surface, the pad pulls water outward, well beyond what looks wet. It slips beneath walls into neighboring rooms while the tack strip begins to absorb it.

Hours 24 to 48

Smell and Mold Start

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Days 3 through 7

Carpet Starts Failing

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

Past one week

Past Saving

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.
