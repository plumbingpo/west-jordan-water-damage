A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. When it happens in West Jordan, West Jordan Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

Common Sewage Cleanup Questions

In West Jordan, a sewage cleanup usually runs $2,000 to $10,000 or more, and the total reflects how large the affected area is and how far contamination spread. Expect roughly $1,500 to $3,000 when the backup is minor and contained, and more than $15,000 in cases of extensive flooding that need materials replaced and rebuilding done.
In most cases our advice is to relocate temporarily while the cleanup happens, above all when contamination is widespread. Both the decontamination chemicals and pathogens in the air can be a risk to your health. Our recommendation will depend on what we find in your particular home.
The emergency phase, meaning sewage removal plus decontamination, generally lasts 1 to 3 days. Complete restoration, including material replacement, may take 1 to 3 weeks depending on the extent of damage. Throughout, the focus is on returning your home to a safe, livable state without delay.
Most backups start with a blockage in the home's sewer line. Grease buildup, wipes and other items that should not be flushed, and tree roots growing into the pipe are the usual causes. Sometimes an aging pipe cracks or caves in, or a storm overwhelms the city sewer and forces sewage up the floor drains. To know for certain, have the line camera-inspected once cleanup is finished.
Without a specific water or sewage backup endorsement, a standard homeowner's policy often will not cover this. The coverage usually runs only $50 to $100 per year and gives critical protection, so our strong recommendation is to add it. Your agent or the policy itself can tell you what you have.

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room
  • Strong sewage or rotten egg odors coming from drains or lower levels
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue

Why Hire a Professional for Sewage Cleanup?

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Schedule a camera inspection of the sewer line every 2 to 3 years so trouble is found early
  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open
  • Know the location and condition of your sewer cleanout for emergency access
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
  • Install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent backups from the municipal system
  • Wipes clog sewer lines even when the label says flushable, so only toilet paper goes down the toilet
