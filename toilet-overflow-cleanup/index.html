Dealing With Toilet Overflow Cleanup in West Jordan

A toilet overflow looks like a mop job, but the water does not stay on the tile. It runs under the baseboard, into the wall and through the gap around the drain pipe. The contents of that water matter more than anything. Water from the tank or supply line is clean. Water that came up through the bowl is not, and it has to be handled as contaminated.

In West Jordan, we begin each overflow job by determining the category of water, which decides how the rest is done. With clean water, the job is extraction followed by drying. Black or gray water calls for protective equipment, taking out porous materials that got soaked, carpet pad for example, and disinfecting everything it contacted. After that, moisture meters go on the wall bases, the subfloor and the room below to make sure no wet spot remains.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Stop the WaterFind the shutoff valve behind the toilet and turn it clockwise as far as it goes. When the valve is frozen, remove the lid of the tank and hold the float up so filling stops.
  2. Stop FlushingNo more flushing, and no running the washer, a shower or a sink. With a blocked drain line, whatever goes down a drain may return through the toilet.
  3. Avoid Dirty WaterWhen there is waste in the water or it came out of the bowl, keep pets and children away, and put on waterproof shoes and gloves before stepping in it.
  4. Check the Room BelowIf the toilet is upstairs, go check the ceiling directly below it. Should you see drips or a sag, cut the power to the room and get belongings out from under it.
  5. Contain Clean WaterLay down old towels so clean water does not travel into hallways or onto carpet. Contaminated water should be left to a crew that has the right disinfectants and equipment.
Insurance

What About Insurance?

When an overflow is accidental and sudden, like a clog nobody expected or a broken fill valve, homeowners policies often cover it. Water that backs up from the sewer or a drain is often excluded unless you added a backup endorsement, and that can apply to an overflow caused by a blocked main line. Months of leaking around the base of a toilet usually falls outside coverage. To learn which of these applies in your case, check the policy or phone your agent.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Roots growing into the sewer line, or a section that has collapsed, slowing all the drains.
  • Waste rising out of the lowest toilet in the house because the main sewer line is blocked.
  • Fill valves or flappers that stick, so the tank never stops running.
  • Failed wax rings under the toilet, which leak at the floor on each flush.
  • Supply lines that give out or tanks that crack, releasing clean water across the floor.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Baseboard and Door Trim Damage

The bottom edge of a baseboard or door casing draws in water as soon as the floor floods. Since trim is usually fiberboard, it puffs up, cracks apart and stays deformed.

Subfloor Saturation

Grout lines and the base of the toilet let water pass under vinyl and tile. Once wet, the plywood subfloor swells, its layers separate, and the floor covering bonded on top can come loose.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

Water from an upstairs bathroom runs down the drain pipe and sits on the drywall ceiling underneath. Stains and sagging follow, and now and then the ceiling comes down.

Bacteria in Building Materials

Water from the drain or bowl deposits bacteria in drywall, baseboards, carpet and pad. Anything porous is normally removed, since it cannot be disinfected with confidence.

We set drying equipment in the bathroom and in the room underneath if water reached it. Our crew keeps metering the subfloor and the ceiling until both read normal again.
