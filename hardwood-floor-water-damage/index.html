Hardwood Floor Water Damage Help in West Jordan

Wood floors react to water quickly. As the wood absorbs water along its edges and bottom face, it expands, crowds the neighboring boards and cups, leaving the center lower than the sides. Cupping alone is no reason to give up on the floor. Boards of solid wood often return to flat if they are dried during those early days. What matters is removing moisture from the wood and from its subfloor steadily and under control.

Our tool for hardwood is the floor drying mat. The mats are sealed to the surface and connected to a vacuum unit that draws moisture up through the boards and seams, while dehumidifiers keep the room air dry. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. No sanding should happen before the wood has dried completely. If cupped boards are sanded while still wet, crowning shows up down the road.

Warning Signs

  • Swollen trim along the edge of the floor near the leak
  • A hazy, whitened or flaking finish where the water sat
  • Gaps opening between boards after a floor has dried too fast
  • Boards lifting off the subfloor or tenting upward at the seams
  • Cupping, where each board's edges rise above its middle
  • Planks with a rounded hump down the center and lower sides
Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Cracks From Rushed Drying

Heavy airflow or heat dries the face of a board well ahead of its core. The result can be cracks, splits and permanent gaps between boards.

Mold Under Boards

Between the boards and the subfloor there is only darkness and still air. Mold in that layer cannot be seen from the room, and a musty odor is usually the first sign.

Water That Keeps Moving

Once below the boards, water moves. Next door rooms, the area under cabinets and walls, and the ceiling below are all places it can end up.

Wet Subfloor

Long after the boards seem dry, the subfloor may still be holding water. If it is not dried too, the floor will cup again or grow mold from underneath.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Water Goes Under

Seams let the water through, and it fans out in the layer between flooring and subfloor. Underneath, the wood remains wet even after the face has been dried.

Hours 24 to 48

Edges Begin to Rise

Boards swell and the edges rise. A cloudy look can appear in the finish. Mold can begin on the subfloor and the unfinished underside of the wood.

Days 3 through 7

Pressure Builds Up

Swelling builds pressure across the floor. Boards crush against each other, fasteners loosen and sections can begin to lift off the subfloor.

Past one week

Permanent Damage

Buckling, black staining and mold under the boards become likely. Drying is usually no longer enough, and the bulk of the wet flooring has to be replaced.

How We Fix It

Our Hardwood Floor Water Damage Process

1

Take Baseline Readings

Our team meters the wet boards, the subfloor under them and a dry area of floor to compare against. The comparison shows how far down moisture reached and how likely the floor is to recover.

2

Extract Standing Water

Standing water is extracted right away and rugs and furniture are moved off the wet area. Boards swell less when the seams stop taking in water sooner.

3

Set Drying Mats

We tape drying mats to the floor and attach them to a vacuum system, which draws moisture upward through the wood. The room is managed with dehumidifiers so drying stays even and the boards do not crack.

4

Track Progress and Report

Every day or two we take new readings, continuing until the subfloor and boards match the dry reference spots. Then we tell you whether the floor needs refinishing, a few boards replaced, or nothing.
