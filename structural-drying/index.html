Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

West Jordan Restoration employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Daily monitoring is built into how we dry a structure, and it relies on calibrated meters, thermal cameras and thermo-hygrometers. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

Signs You Need Structural Drying in West Jordan

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Beads of moisture on windows and cold surfaces after the water event
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • Drywall that is soft, swollen or shows moisture staining
  • Earlier water damage that was never professionally dried and now shows deterioration
  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Keep windows and doors closed in areas being dried so the dehumidifiers stay efficient
  • Daily monitoring only works if someone can let the technician into the property
  • Deal with water intrusion right away, since an early start on drying gives a better result
  • Each unit is positioned according to the moisture map, so ask before shifting anything
  • If a new damp spot or smell shows up while drying is underway, let the technician know at once
  • Go by the meter, not by touch, since a dry-feeling surface may still hold moisture deep inside

Why Structural Drying Is Not a DIY Job

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Get it wrong in one direction and mold feeds on leftover moisture, get it wrong in the other and wood or similar materials are damaged by over-drying.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.
