AC Leak Water Damage Help in West Jordan

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. Our team measures how far it traveled, dries the building materials and fixes the damage. Clearing the drain or repairing the unit remains a job for an HVAC technician, and our schedule can fit around theirs.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut the System DownSwitch the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalUntil that area's power has been shut off, keep your hands away from the unit and from any outlet or ceiling light with water in or near it.
  3. Clear Water From the PanIf you can reach the pan safely, soak up or wet-vac the standing water so it stops spilling over. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Call Both TradesYou need two calls: one to us so we can measure the wet area, one to an HVAC technician who will unclog the drain. Keep the AC off until the repair is made.
How We Fix It

Our AC Leak Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

We use moisture meters and thermal imaging around the air handler, the ceiling or walls next to it, and the flooring outside the closet to find every wet material, not only the stained ones.

2

Extract and Open Up

Our extractors pull water out of the pan, the wood platform and any carpet. Drywall or insulation that is saturated or moldy is cut out so the cavity behind it can air out.

3

Dry the Structure

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, usually 3 to 5, while we track the readings. We place equipment so the HVAC technician can still reach the unit.

4

Clean and Restore

Since condensate left standing for days is seldom clean, we apply an antimicrobial to the surfaces it touched. Then we replace insulation, drywall, baseboard and flooring and repaint so the area matches.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

With AC leaks, insurers mostly look at how long the water was running. If the pan overflowed suddenly and you acted when you found it, many homeowners policies pay for the resulting water damage. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Coil icing brought on by a filter that was not changed or a refrigerant charge that is low.
  • Water escaping from a drain pan that has cracked, rusted through or tilted.
  • Algae, slime and dust from the coil plugging the condensate drain.
  • A drain line joint that came apart or was never glued, leaking inside a wall or attic.
  • Units that rely on a condensate pump, when that pump quits or gets unplugged.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Attic Insulation

Batts or blown-in material next to the pan absorb the leak and press that moisture onto the ceiling. Once saturated it stops insulating, and replacement is common.

Closet Walls and Platform

Closet units often sit on a wood platform over the return air box. The wood rots and the nearby drywall draws moisture upward, all of it out of sight in back of the unit.

Drywall Below the Air Handler

Overflow from a unit in the attic lands on the ceiling drywall. Staining is the early sign, sagging follows, and drywall left wet for days can come down into the room below.

Mold Near the Return

Mold may appear in 24 to 48 hours on damp materials by the air handler, where the return air stream can carry its spores through the ductwork to other rooms.
