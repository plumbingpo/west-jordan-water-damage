Kitchen Water Damage Help in West Jordan

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. For homeowners in West Jordan, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

What It Looks Like

  • Side panels on the base cabinets that are flaking or swollen at the bottom
  • The toe kick boards have puffed up, turned dark, or come loose
  • Water stains or bubbled paint on the wall behind the sink or dishwasher
  • An unexplained jump in water use, or dripping you can hear when nothing is running
  • Downstairs, a brownish mark on the ceiling beneath the kitchen
  • Doors on the cabinets that have gone out of alignment or started sticking

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First hour

Water Runs Under

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Toe Kick Space

Every side of the space beneath base cabinets is closed off. It gathers water that sits for weeks, and the resulting mold stays hidden unless the cabinets are removed.

The Ice Maker Line

A few drops per minute can escape from the narrow tube that supplies the ice maker. Behind a fridge that never moves, that is enough to rot flooring and the wall behind.

Sink Drain Leaks

A loose drain fitting or disposal seal leaks only while the sink is draining, so it is easy to miss. The water carries food waste, which is why a wet sink cabinet smells and molds so fast.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

The wall at the back of the sink and dishwasher holds drain and supply pipes. Cabinets fully cover that drywall and framing, so a leak can soak them and mold can spread out of sight.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Stop the Source

We shut off the leaking fixture or appliance line, pull the dishwasher or refrigerator if needed, and confirm exactly which connection failed so it can be repaired before drying begins.

2

Measure the Wet Area

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. It is common for water beneath a kitchen floor to spread further than you would guess.

3

Dry Without Demolition

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Remove Only Damage

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.
