Dealing With Water Heater Leak Cleanup in West Jordan

The tank on a water heater stores 40 to 80 gallons, yet a leak can release far more than that. Because the supply line feeds the tank, a rusted out bottom drains and refills at the same pace. Until someone closes the cold water valve, it keeps running. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

Our West Jordan team extracts the water, then looks at what surrounded the heater. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. When the heater is in the garage, items kept on the slab get wet and so does the wall adjoining the house. From an attic it comes down through insulation and ceilings. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut the Power or GasIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. If an adjuster finds signs of a long-term leak, such as rust trails or old staining, the claim can be reduced or denied because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • A temperature and pressure relief valve that opens or fails and discharges water.
  • A drain valve that loosens, cracks or no longer closes all the way.
  • Excess pressure in the tank from a failed thermostat or a missing expansion tank.
  • Anode rods that are used up, leaving the steel inside the tank open to rust.
  • Fittings left slightly loose by recent repair or installation work.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Shared Garage Wall

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Heater Closet Drywall

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. Baseboards swell and the lower wall stays wet inside.

Stand and Subfloor Damage

Under the tank, the wood stand or the subfloor absorbs the largest share of the leak. Rot can weaken it, a real concern once a full new tank sits on top.

Items Stored Near the Heater

Whatever is kept by a closet or garage heater, from keepsakes to furniture and boxes, is the first thing to get wet. Cardboard falls apart, and wet contents can mold if they are not dried or discarded quickly.

What We Do

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Remove Standing Water

Standing water is taken up from the garage, closet or floor. With an attic unit we also clear the ceiling cavity and overflow pan, which keeps nearby materials from soaking up more.

2

Check Around the Heater

To learn the full reach of the water, our crew meters and scans the platform, the bottoms of the walls, neighboring rooms and the ceiling below if there is one.

3

Take Out What Will Not Dry

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. For an attic leak, the wet insulation is cleared out, which lets us dry the drywall and ceiling joists beneath.

4

Drying and Moisture Readings

We keep the drying equipment going for the usual 3 to 5 days needed to dry the space. Moisture readings are recorded, and you hear from us once the space can take the new heater.
