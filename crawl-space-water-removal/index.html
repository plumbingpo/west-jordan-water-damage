Crawl Space Water Removal in West Jordan

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. By then the insulation is usually soaked and the wood above it has been wet for days. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

Signs to Look For

  • Stale, mildew type air coming up around the vents in the floor
  • Fuzzy patches, pale or dark, spreading across the framing or subfloor
  • Ducts and plumbing lines below the house covered in condensation
  • Insulation that has pulled loose and hangs in wet clumps below the joists
  • Flooring overhead that seems chilly, clammy, spongy or springy underfoot
  • Moisture loving pests such as camel crickets and silverfish appearing indoors
Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Air From Below

Because air naturally moves upward from under the home, spores and stale odors in a wet crawl space reach your living areas too.

Shifting Piers and Supports

Long periods of standing water loosen the soil that holds up the support piers. You might notice it as a door that suddenly rubs or a floor that is no longer level.

Contaminated Water

What collects beneath a home is frequently dirty. A leaking drain line or groundwater carrying soil and waste is Category 2 or 3 and needs protective gear and disinfection.

Pests Drawn to Moisture

A wet crawl space offers the soft wood and moisture that wood destroying insects and mice prefer. Once the area is dry it becomes far less inviting.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Inspect and Make Safe

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Remove Wet Materials

We bag up the wet insulation, damaged vapor barrier and loose debris and take it all outside. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Dry and Verify

Compact fans and dehumidification work in the crawl space as long as it takes for the framing to test normal. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First day

Water Pools Below

The dirt floor starts absorbing moisture as puddles form in every dip. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

Hours 24 to 48

Early Mold Growth

Mold can start on damp joists and paper faced insulation. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

Past one week

Wood Starts Rotting

Wood that stays wet begins to decay. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.
