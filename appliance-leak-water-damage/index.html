Appliance Leak Water Damage in West Jordan: What to Know

Most of the appliance leaks we see trace back to three machines. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. A dishwasher tends to leak slowly from a door seal or drain hose, out of sight under the cabinets. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. Cleanup is different for each of them.

On every West Jordan job, the first step is moving the appliance and tracing the path the water took. For a kitchen, the usual places are below the flooring and under the cabinets. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut the Machine DownStop the cycle and unplug the machine if the outlet and floor around it are dry. When either one is wet, turn the power off at the breaker.
  2. Close the Supply ValveBehind a washer, on the wall, are separate hot and cold valves. Dishwashers shut off under the sink. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Mop Up What You CanMop or towel up what you can reach so it stops spreading under cabinets and flooring. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Document What FailedKeep the part that failed, whether a hose, a fitting or a valve, and get photos of it. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.
The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Standpipes that clog, or the drain hose from a washer coming loose from one.
  • Refrigerators that leak at floor level because the drain pan has cracked or the defrost drain is clogged.
  • Plastic or copper ice maker lines that kink, crack or loosen at the fitting.
  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.
  • Too much detergent or an overloaded drum that makes the machine overflow.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Wall Bases and Trim

On a flooded kitchen or laundry floor, the baseboards and the lower edge of the drywall draw water upward. Since it is out of view, the wall behind the machine tends to be wetter than the rest.

Adjoining Rooms

Water may pour from a washer hose for hours on end. It passes under walls into hallways, closets and bedrooms, and soaks carpet and pad well away from the laundry room.

Kitchen Cabinets

Toe kicks and cabinet sides take on water from underneath when a dishwasher leaks. Swelling and separation follow, and for weeks the soaked cabinet boxes may trap moisture against the wall.

Mold Behind Appliances

Hardly anyone looks at the dark, warm space behind a dishwasher or refrigerator. A slow drip there feeds mold on the wall and floor long before anyone notices.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Pull and Inspect

The appliance is disconnected and moved, and the supply is confirmed off. Then thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went below flooring, under the cabinets and into the walls nearby.

2

Extract the Water

Carpet and floors are cleared of standing water with extraction equipment. To reach water caught below cabinets or under floating floors, our crew pulls toe kicks or takes up boards.

3

Dry in Place

Dehumidifiers and air movers are set up, and when it is possible, directed airflow dries hardwood and cabinets where they sit so nothing has to be torn out.

4

Clean and Confirm

Surfaces touched by washer or dishwasher discharge get antimicrobial treatment. Final moisture readings are taken before we return the appliance, so it never sits on a damp floor.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. Gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage, so slow seepage at a dishwasher or an ice maker line makes for a tougher claim. Some insurers also ask about the age and upkeep of the hose. Before cleanup, take photos, save the failed part and read your policy.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • Whether this was a sudden flood or slow seepage, and which machine it came from.
  • If it was gray discharge, which must be sanitized, or clean water from the supply.
  • Duration of the leak, particularly a hidden drip that went unnoticed behind the machine.
  • Presence of mold, which adds removal work in front of the drying.
