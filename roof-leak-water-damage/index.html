Roof Leak Water Damage in West Jordan: What to Know

A roof leak rarely starts where the stain appears. Rain slips under a lifted shingle, a cracked flashing joint or a tired skylight seal, then runs along the decking and rafters before it drops onto the insulation. Often the attic has been damp for some time before any brown mark reaches the room below. Our team maps everything that got wet, then dries it.

To be clear, roof repair is not what we do, water damage is. Inside the home, our team takes out saturated insulation, dries the wood and drywall, treats any mold and repairs the ceiling. Should the leak still be active, our crew can cover that section with a tarp for now, or time our work with the roofing contractor you pick so nothing gets sealed over an open leak.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Cut Power to Wet FixturesIf water is dripping through a light fixture, a ceiling fan or near an outlet, switch off that circuit at the breaker and leave the fixture alone.
  2. Contain the WaterSet buckets or pans under active drips and move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the way. Lay towels or plastic sheeting down to protect the floor.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeWhen water pools in a bubble overhead, pierce the low point with a screwdriver while standing clear, and let it run into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. Note from inside where the water is coming in and leave the rest to professionals.
  5. Photograph the DamageBefore anything is moved or discarded, get video and photos of the attic, the ceiling and damaged items, and then phone us so drying can begin.
Insurance

Insurance and Roof Leak Water Damage

Interior damage from a sudden, accidental roof leak, like one caused by a fallen limb or wind-torn shingles, is often covered under a homeowners policy. Insurers generally turn down claims tied to wear, age or lack of upkeep, and they may handle the roof repair on different terms than the inside damage. We document the damage with photos and moisture readings for your adjuster, but coverage depends on your policy, so read it or ask your agent.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Corroded or loose metal flashing where the roof meets a chimney, a vent or a wall.
  • Missing or broken shingles, often after high wind, with bare underlayment beneath.
  • Sun-cracked rubber collars where plumbing vent pipes come through the roof.
  • Nails that have backed out of the decking and left small holes through the shingles.
  • Clogged gutters that back water up under the shingles at the roof edge.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Ceiling Drywall

Staining, sagging and loss of strength follow when drywall is not dried. Seams split, the tape lets go, and a heavy enough section can drop with little warning.

Wall Cavities

Rain sometimes follows the framing into a wall, where the insulation and the hidden side of the drywall take it up. You might notice nothing more than a peeling patch or a puffy baseboard.

Rafters and Decking

Wood in the roof structure that is soaked over and over darkens, swells and softens. Once they rot, the frame supporting the roof is weaker.

Mold in the Attic

A dark, warm attic with damp wood and paper-faced drywall suits mold well. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours and spread across the underside of the decking.
