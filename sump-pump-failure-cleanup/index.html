Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Help in West Jordan

Sump pumps do a single task, yet they commonly quit right when that task matters. Heavy rain raises the water table, the pit fills, and then the power goes out, the float sticks or the pump cannot keep up. From there it tops the edge of the basin and runs out over the slab. Until pumping resumes, the water continues to rise.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. That changes the cleanup: porous materials like carpet pad and wet drywall usually come out, and everything left is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stay Out of the WaterDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsTurn off the basement circuits at the breaker panel, but only if you can reach it from a dry spot. A panel surrounded by water is a job for the power company.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Do not reach into the water.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Move Valuables and CallGet dry valuables to a higher floor, snap photos showing the pump and how high the water is, and give us a call. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

Sump pump losses are widely misunderstood when it comes to insurance. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Often the only coverage comes from an added endorsement for water backup and sump overflow, which has limits of its own. Check the declarations page for it, or call your agent to find out. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.

Our Process

Our Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Process

1

Get the Water Out

We bring our own pumps and extractors, so nothing depends on your failed sump. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Remove Contaminated Materials

Wearing protective gear, we take out the carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that the groundwater touched, bag it and haul it away so clean framing and concrete are exposed.

3

Clean and Sanitize

The slab, wall framing and remaining contents are washed down and treated with an antimicrobial product. Doing it ahead of drying keeps the air movers from spreading contaminants through the basement.

4

Dry and Confirm

We run drying equipment until our meters show normal moisture in the slab and the wood, which typically takes 3 to 5 days. A working pump should be back in the pit before our equipment leaves.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • A failed check valve that lets pumped water run back into the pit.
  • More water flowing into the pit than an undersized pump can move.
  • A float switch that is stuck, tangled or pinned against the basin wall.
  • The pump being unplugged by accident or sitting on an outlet that tripped.
  • Losing electricity in a storm when the pump has no battery backup.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Stored Belongings

Cardboard boxes, furniture legs, books and fabrics soak up contaminated water quickly. Anything porous that sat in it is hard to disinfect and will grow mold within days.

Furnace and Water Heater

Mechanical equipment often shares the floor with the sump pit. Water reaching burners, controls or wiring can ruin them, and each appliance must be inspected before it is used again.

Low Electrical Outlets

Wall outlets near the floor are sometimes submerged in a sump flood. Before those circuits are used again, an electrician needs to inspect whatever was under water.

Finished Basement Walls

The wet zone inside a wall reaches well past the water mark, as drywall and insulation pull moisture up off the floor. The lower part that got wet is cut away, not dried, on account of the contamination.
