Bathroom Water Damage Help in West Jordan

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. In West Jordan, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Signs to Look For

  • The bottom panel inside the vanity has swelled, stained or bowed
  • A hollow sound, movement, or loose tiles in the floor by the bathtub
  • Directly under the bathroom, a ceiling with a brown stain or a sag
  • Dark stains at the base of the toilet, or a bowl that wobbles a little
  • A spongy feel in the floor beside the tub or in front of the vanity
  • The room keeps a damp, musty smell even right after a thorough cleaning
The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First days

Water Gets Past

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

First few weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

The wood under the tile never gets a chance to dry between showers. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months or more

Structure Weakens

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Slow Vanity Drips

Supply valves and drain fittings under the sink can drip for months behind stored bottles and towels. The cabinet floor swells, and the water runs through to the subfloor and the wall behind it.

Damage Downstairs

Water follows pipes and joists before it drips, so the stain downstairs may sit several feet from the actual leak. Its path can cross electrical wiring and ceiling lights.

Trapped Steam

Steam lingers after each shower when the exhaust fan is weak or absent. Mold feeds on the moisture that lands on ceilings and walls, and damp materials dry far more slowly.

Mold in the Tiled Wall

By themselves, grout and tile do not keep water out. Mold takes hold on the wall board once that hidden layer remains wet, and no scrubbing can get to it.

What We Do

How We Handle Bathroom Water Damage

1

Pinpoint the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Soft wall board, loose tile and subfloor that has rotted are removed. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Cavities

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. Nothing is sealed back up until we have treated with antimicrobial and the readings show dry.

4

Rebuild and Seal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.
